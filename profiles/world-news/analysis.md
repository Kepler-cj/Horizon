# Evaluation goal

Evaluate the importance of a piece of international news for a reader who wants to know what actually matters in the world that day, independent of their professional field.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Historic.** Events with global consequences that will be referenced for years: war escalation or ceasefire, a change of government in a major power, a systemic financial or energy shock, a treaty or sanctions regime that reshapes relations.
- **7-8: Major.** Significant developments with clear cross-border or nationwide consequences: major policy shifts, large-scale military or diplomatic moves, central bank decisions, natural disasters with heavy casualties, landmark court or regulatory rulings.
- **5-6: Notable.** Developments worth knowing about that are limited in scope: regional politics, mid-sized economic data, a single country's domestic policy, follow-ups to earlier major events.
- **3-4: Low priority.** Routine political process, incremental updates, speculation and commentary without new facts, single-company business news.
- **0-2: Noise.** Sensationalism, unverified claims, tabloid content, celebrity or entertainment coverage, purely promotional material.

# Evaluation guidance

Score the scale and concreteness of the consequence, not how dramatic the headline sounds. Weigh how many people or countries are affected, whether the change is durable or reversible, whether named institutions or state actors are involved, and whether the report contains verifiable specifics such as numbers, dates, places, or named officials. Do not treat a country's general prominence as evidence that a specific story matters. Do not reward speculation, opinion pieces, or repeated coverage of an old event.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
